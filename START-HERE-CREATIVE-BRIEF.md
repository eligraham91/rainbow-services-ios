# Start Here — Creative Brief
### National Domestic Violence Support App
**Built by Rainbow Services · San Pedro, Los Angeles, CA**

---

## What this is

**Start Here** is a national, trauma-informed mobile app that gives survivors of domestic violence and people who care about them the tools to understand abuse, build a personal safety plan, find local programs, and act in a crisis — privately, with no account required.

It is not a Rainbow Services intake tool. Rainbow Services is the builder and funder. The app serves anyone, anywhere in the United States.

---

## The problem it solves

When someone is experiencing domestic violence — or suspects someone they love is — the path from "I think something is wrong" to "I know what to do" is invisible. Finding a shelter means Googling from a shared device. Understanding whether what's happening counts as abuse requires navigating clinical language. Building a safety plan requires knowing what a safety plan is.

This app makes that path visible, private, and human.

---

## Who it's for

**Primary:** Survivors of domestic violence. Any gender, any age, any background. Urgency level ranges from "in immediate danger right now" to "I've been trying to understand my situation for months."

**Secondary:** People helping a survivor. A friend, sibling, coworker, neighbor who doesn't know how to help without making things worse.

**Tertiary:** Professionals (advocates, social workers) who want to hand survivors a tool they can keep on their phone.

## Core features

### 1. Emergency triage
The first question the app answers: *I need help right now.* Surfaces 911, the National DV Hotline (1-800-799-7233), and local crisis lines. Triage chips route to next steps based on what the person actually needs in the moment.

### 2. Education: understanding domestic violence
Plain language explanations of what DV is, the types of abuse (physical, emotional, financial, digital), and the patterns of power and control (Duluth Model). Self-reflection prompts framed as general information — never a diagnosis. The read-out is: "Speaking with an advocate can help" — not "you are being abused."

### 3. Supporter guide
A dedicated section for people helping someone else. What helps, what doesn't, how to stay safe yourself, and how to find local resources for the person you're trying to support.

### 4. Safety plan builder
A 6-step guided wizard that saves locally, encrypted on the device: escape routes, documents to grab, emergency contacts, code word, safe places, finances. Export as a plain-text PDF with no app name in the header (so the document doesn't identify itself on a shared device).

### 5. National shelter finder
3,681 DV programs across all 50 states and territories, sourced from FVPSA federal grant data. Search by ZIP (radius-expanding from 25 to 100 miles), city/state, or state name. National DV Hotline always pinned at the top. Confidential shelter addresses are never shown. Service filter chips: Emergency Shelter, Hotline, Legal, Housing, Children & Family, Spanish-language, LGBTQ+, Pet-inclusive, Immigration.

**Safe List:** bookmark any program, then copy the full list as plain text to paste into a message or notes app — with no "generated by" header that could identify the source.

### 6. Encrypted vault
Biometric-locked (Face ID / Touch ID) private note storage for anything the user wants to keep off their main Notes app. Safety plan lives here. Access auto-locks when the app backgrounds.

### 7. Privacy architecture
- No account, no login, no cloud sync
- All data stored on-device, encrypted with AES-256 (MMKV + iOS Keychain)
- No analytics on sensitive screens
- Shake to exit: three shakes → instant redirect to a neutral screen, clears navigation history
- Background masking: when the app backgrounds, the screen is replaced with a blank overlay so the app switcher doesn't reveal content
- App camouflage profile (EAS build variant): can ship as "Calculator" with a neutral icon

## Design system

**Aesthetic:** Swiss editorial grid. Premium, restrained. Deliberately not a "shelter nonprofit" visual aesthetic — no stock imagery, no blue gradient, no red cross.

**Palette:**
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `creamBase` | `#F5F1E8` | App background |
| `creamCard` | `#EDE4CE` | Raised surfaces, GlassCards |
| `inkPrimary` | `#1A1A1A` | Headlines, body |
| `inkMuted` | `#4A4A4A` | Captions, secondary text |
| `purpleAnchor` | `#4A148C` | Brand purple, CTAs, links |
| `safetyRed` | `#C62828` | **DV safety messaging only.** Never for generic UI danger. |
| `ruleLine` | `#C4B99E` | Dividers, card edges |

**Typography:**
- **Inter Tight** — display, headings (weight 700–900, tight letter-spacing, large at −1.5px)
- **Inter** — body, UI labels (400–600)
- **JetBrains Mono** — eyebrows, data labels, timestamps (10–12px, uppercase, 1.3px letter-spacing)

**Motion:** All animations run on the UI thread via react-native-reanimated worklets. Spring physics (stiffness 180, damping 20) for entrances. `useTraumaInformedMotion()` reduces animation for users with Reduce Motion enabled. No gratuitous motion — restrained, purposeful.

**Cards:** Glassmorphic via `expo-blur` (`BlurView`, `intensity={20}`). Cream-on-cream layering. 12px border radius, 1px `ruleLine` border.

**Background:** Full-bleed animated mesh gradient driven by gyroscope (`expo-sensors`), rendered via `@shopify/react-native-skia`. Slow, organic movement.

## Voice and tone

**The frame:** Survivor agency. Not rescue narrative. Not "we help victims." The app gives tools; the person uses them on their own terms.

**Spare. Direct. Earned.** No filler phrases, no clinical hedging, no chatbot warmth.

**Hard rules:**
- Never: "rescue," "save," "help victims," "at-risk individuals"
- Always: "survivors," "people experiencing DV," "serve," "support"
- Never: em dashes in body copy — use a period or parentheses
- Never: AI-tell words ("leverage," "holistic," "robust," "delve")
- Safety red is for safety messaging only, never for generic warnings
- "CALL 911 NOW" — exact string, no variation
- Oxford comma always

## Safety guardrails (non-negotiable)

These apply to every screen, every feature, every future build:

1. **No device geolocation prompt.** Users type a ZIP or city. No `requestWhenInUseAuthorization`.
2. **Shelter addresses never displayed for confidential locations.** VAWA §12291(b)(2). Only city + state for restricted records.
3. **National DV Hotline always reachable** — pinned above search results regardless of query state.
4. **Two taps to call from a result card.** Card tap → callout. Callout has the call button. Never auto-dial.
5. **No analytics on sensitive screens** — /resources, /plan, /vault, /emergency. No events, no funnels.
6. **Quick Exit always present.** The floating pill has a red × panic button → `/stealth` in one tap + Warning haptic.
7. **No clipboard copy attributing the app.** The Safe List copy format contains program data only, no "Generated by Start Here" header.
8. **Mandatory-reporting caveat** on any screen claiming confidentiality.
